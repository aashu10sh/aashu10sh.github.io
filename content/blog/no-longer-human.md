This book was a breath of fresh air.

What I really liked about this book is about how yozo is an unreliable narrator, he is not a unreliable narrator in the traditional sense that he gets the events mixed up or hullucinates things but how he is unaware of his own humanity - he has decided that is not a human being and he does not belong at a very young age.

This book has made me ask one profound question? how do you know you do not like something without trying that something first? when a child claims not like avacados without ever trying avacados in the first place, do they ever know if they really not like avacados? similarly how do you know that you are not a human without first trying to be human? This begs the question if anyone ever is human in the first place? is being human something we are all born with or maybe it is something we grow into with life. If that is the case then can other animals raised by humans ever be humans? Ah! the classic nature vs nurture! there is no escaping this one, is it?

With Yozo, I wonder why he decided he was not human because I am certain that every human being has felt the way he has, as he describes it in the book, so really its all just overthinking?, he believes its a feeling exclusive to him but does not realise that everyone is the same ( he does suspect with Hiroki ) The way the bar lady talks about him in the epilogue makes this starkly clear, she calls him "The yozo we knew was so easy going and amusing and only if he hadn't drunk - no, even though he did drink - he was a good boy, an angel." Which means either he was not human and he succeded in fooling everyone for 30 years OR he was human and that his twisted sense of self corrupted him into thinking he was not.

I really liked how Yozo was not a serial killer or did not like violence/killing, it is so easy to write boring characters under the "loner", "alienation" archetype, give them a sad story and make them a killer, how original! Happy that Dazai chose not to make yozo pretentious like that. 

We all have a bit of Yozo in us and it is paramount that we remain mostly human.
