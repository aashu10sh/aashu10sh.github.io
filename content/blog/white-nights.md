## Aashutosh's Review
This story is a clear example of how unreliable human emotions really are. Human emotions are like colors on a canvas of an abstract painting. There is no structure and meaning (maybe some) to them, they come and they go and in various unexplainable shapes and sizes. Often times the human is unsure of what emotions they are feeling or are supposed to feel, one clear example of this is how: you think how you are supposed to feel cathartic joy and bliss after taking revenge on someone who wronged you but you are often left with nothing but a deep sense of sadness and unfulfillment.

This is the human condition, cursed with the inability to understand one's own emotional state and given the illusion that one can control said state.

This condition is made cristal clear by both the leads of our story: the dreamer and Nastenka. The dreamer is lonely and often dreams of a life with someone, he yearns for love and connection and has idiolised a figure in his mind for which he will fall in love with, he has already in his mind made up the characteristics his lover will have and what she will be like. 

He is, in every sense of the word a **Yearner**, yearning for someone madeup, an ideal entity, that will not match his expectations in the real world. A yearner like the dreamer fails to account for the fact that we are humans and by that very definition we are deeply flawed and no one is perfect. Nastenka is very flawed herself, she waits patiently for a man who may or may not come, for a man who may or may not have cheated her, she tells herself that she loves him deeply but if he had not shown she was willing to love the dreamer and live a life with him. She knows she feels love ( or does she? ) but she does not know who she feels this love for. Just like the dreamer, her lover has become a almost fictitious idol and a perfect entity in her mind. 

This is what dostoevsky is trying to highlight to the readers in this book. A problem with modern love where we are not in love with a person but in love with the idiolized unrealistic idea of a person. A situation where the identity of your lover becomes background information and what they are in your mind become predominant. This was Dostovesky's warning to us. If you are going to love someone, love the person, the individual, not the ideal they represent.

Both our characters have dwelled too long in the land of the dreams and allowed mistaking a dreamt perfect idiolized version with the real deal and this has been their biggest sin. Its important to dream but its also important that we come down to grasslands once in a while.


## Rashihang's Review

To make this not as long, going directly into things I want to discuss,

My thoughts on white nights and its nature is that, it really isn’t a “love story” or a story about “unrequited love” .I don’t really think the main character really loves Nashtenka like he says he does. It is a story more so about lonely and socially inept individuals that want so desperately to cling onto anyone as to not feel isolated and abandoned. 
I think this because when in my 2nd shift through of this book, after the first read. I wanted to recollect what had drove the mc into such a deep attraction and strong feelings towards Nashtenka. I wanted something like a beautiful depiction of Nashtenka told by the mc when first seeing her but in the book everything he describes about her is so mundane and you can feel by the writing or the narration of his that he really feelings are not really that strong with her up to a certain point later on. 
I think this depends on your definition of love which also is varies from people to people but in no part of this book do I think Nastenka is special for him. 
What I mean by that will be cleared up further.
Like I said the MC doesn’t have that strong of feelings reflected in his narration towards Nastenka up to a certain point, which the certain point is I think when Nastenka asks him about himself. This is very revealing in terms of how Dostoevsky writes his characters, talking about Notes from Underground and not spoiling too much, most of Dostoevsky characters are deeply lost individuals, the narrators more than anyone. Both of them share a similar incident in both of their stories, which is when faced with an opportunity to be open about themselves, they go onto ramble about their selves that is not really true or is really disingenuous but at the same time they would want it to be true, they want themselves to be honest in the sense that the depictions that they have for them, even if it may be imaginary is honest to themselves. In thought, they do think that if given the chance, they would be what they say they are. But it is not the case. 
Which is I think the biggest tragedy in Dostoevsky’s stories, these characters are not themselves and any attempt to find and be their true selves leaves them in a worse predicament than before.
Like the MCs ramblings of the “dreamers”.
Before even that I would want to discuss his cold and isolating loneliness he has experienced before coming across Nashtenka, we do not hear it from him directly which adds to his disingenuous narration, we only see the symptoms and results of his isolation. He shows signs of it by his weird fixation on the city walls and colors of his surrounding buildings, his fixation on the old man he only comes across once a day which he mentions never talking to and his closest human interaction being the tipping of both their hats and their shared glances. From that we can only piece together that, he is so starved of a social bond with other people that event something you and I would forget about is a day or two is a found routine of his. We can tell that he certainly isn’t taking the isolation well because of his distaste for people he shows in the first lines of the book.
Because of these points I would describe the mc as “lost” meaning he himself does not know who he is and what it is that he wants. Was it truly ever about Nashtenka? Or was the desire within more selfish, about himself almost.
Would any other woman in that same bridge under the same circumstance have invoked the strong attraction that the mc had? I think so. 
To discuss this further, we have to talk about Nashtenka, starting with why she was so important to the mc, when the mc started to fall in love with her and how it came about that she had IMO done the mc more of a favor than destroyed him like I initially thought.
Earlier I had talked about how I didn’t think the mc loved Nestenka at first but only after she asked him about himself. Which is turn for the first time allowed him to talk about himself, or at least what he thought of himself. As a loner with not some many chances to talk about himself he does start to find himself in his ramblings, which then leads him to value Nastenka. After we know about Nastenkas history, we as the reader get to know that she is also someone that was deprived of social interactions up to a normal standard at least. There we see that the mc for the first time gets to talk with someone who might be in the same boat as he is, she feels just as alone as he does and longs for companionship just of much as he does. It is not a matter to both of them WHO it is, as long as it keeps them from being alone.
I think it is demonstrated in Nastenkas choice, and I make both the mc and Nashtenka out to be almost similar as people in that sense that they both would fall in love with the first person that they get close to, to make their loneliness disappear. With that I do ask, is it right? What Nashtenka did to the mc? Asking him not to fall in love with her when I think she’d make the same mistake as the mc?  In this way I think Nastenka already knew the core issue they both faced where Nastenka had already found an end to her loneliness, she knew she was the end to mc’s. That is what she so desperately didn’t want to be the one who gave him something of a false hope but at the same time because she’s already been feeling abandoned used the mc to recover from it.  
And that is why I think Nastenka made the choice that she did and the part that reveals that both the mc and her were really the same person, it’s because they found the person they wish they were only alongside their partners, Nastenka in her first love with the other guy and our mc with Nastenka. Both rather selfish in their reasons but unknown to them.

In the end when the mc talks about how the feeling had left him imagining his room and everything around him growing older and the outside city feeling more miserable, I cannot help but contrast it against what he said during what would be his final night with nastenka, how hopeful he was for the future and how drastic of change in life he was inviting and in an instance they were are faint dreams, also with how his view of the city around him had become more jaded and cynical in terms of the change, or much rather I would describe them as more realistic depictions of his living. No longer is he the same dreamer before he met her and that I think is a better thing for him. He is not “destroyed” by Nastenka but rather taken out of his cocoon. And his memory of her still being so fond and grateful to her with casting any sort of remorse away from that leads me to believe, he has changed for the better.

One thing of note that I want to say about Dostoevsky is that in both Notes from the Underground and White Nights, the main characters are both very lost individuals that don’t have a strong identity of themselves while at the same time having thoughts about themselves as the contrary, as honest and intellectual men who know what others think, how people behave and have in their heart while themselves not knowing what are or desire. Intelligence becomes a mask in Notes for the MC while this Naivety becomes the mask for the MC in White Nights. When thinking about it long enough. They do seem like the same person. There are many other shared traits like the outburst of rambling as I mentioned earlier whenever questioned about themselves and both of them being disingenuous witnesses of their own character. While in Notes, I found the sheer pitifulness of the mc hilarious and his honestly brutal confessions enjoyable, in white nights I feel pity for the mc in his earlier naivety with his earlier narration feeling disingenuous and only at the end finally some honesty from him.
